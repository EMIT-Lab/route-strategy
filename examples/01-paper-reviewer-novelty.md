# Worked Example 01 — Reviewer says the paper lacks novelty

## User request

> Reviewer 2 says our novelty is weak and the method looks like an incremental combination of existing approaches. Help me write a strong rebuttal.

---

## Default execution-first response

A conventional assistant may immediately:

1. thank the reviewer;
2. list differences from prior work;
3. use stronger novelty language;
4. draft a persuasive response letter.

This can produce excellent prose while failing the actual task.

---

# ROUTE analysis

## R — Reframe

**Surface task:** Write a strong rebuttal.

**Possible real objective:** Get the reviewer/editor to accept that the paper contains a defensible, meaningful contribution.

The reviewer's criticism may not be a "wording problem." It may signal one of four deeper problems:

1. the contribution is genuinely incremental;
2. the contribution is real but the manuscript frames the wrong novelty;
3. the evidence does not isolate the claimed novelty;
4. the contribution is meaningful to a different audience than the manuscript currently targets.

Therefore, writing the rebuttal first may be premature.

---

## O — Outcome

**Key stakeholder:** Reviewer and, ultimately, the handling editor.

**Success criterion:** After revision, a skeptical reader can answer:

- What becomes possible because of this work that was not possible before?
- What is conceptually new rather than merely technically different?
- Which experiment/analysis demonstrates that difference?
- Why does the difference matter to the journal's audience?

**Non-goal:** "Win the argument" through rhetoric alone.

---

## U — Understand the game

A reviewer calling work "incremental" often evaluates novelty at a different level than the authors.

The authors may be defending:

> "Our network architecture is different."

The reviewer may be asking:

> "Does this difference create a new scientific capability or insight?"

The relevant unit of novelty may therefore be:
- scientific question;
- inference capability;
- experimental workflow;
- interpretability;
- generality;
- evidence quality;
rather than architecture.

---

## T — Test

### Framing test
If the paper removed the new architecture name, would the central scientific contribution remain?

### Failure test
Could the authors produce a beautifully written rebuttal and still lose because the figures do not demonstrate the claimed advantage?

### Evidence test
What result isolates the claimed contribution from:
- more parameters;
- more training data;
- a better preprocessing pipeline;
- a different benchmark split?

### Alternative routes

**Route A — Rhetorical defense only**  
Fast, but weak if the reviewer diagnosed a real manuscript problem.

**Route B — Reframe + targeted evidence**  
Identify the actual scientific contribution, revise title/abstract/figures accordingly, and add the minimum decisive comparison or ablation.

**Route C — Narrow the claim**  
If the reviewer is correct, reduce novelty claims and reposition the work around a more defensible contribution.

### Recommended route
Usually **B**, unless falsification shows the central claim cannot survive.

---

# Strategy Contract

**Surface task:** Draft rebuttal.  
**Real objective:** Make the manuscript's contribution defensible to a skeptical evaluator.  
**Key stakeholder:** Reviewer + handling editor.  
**Success criterion:** The revised paper demonstrates a meaningful capability/insight unavailable from the closest alternatives.  
**Strategic risk:** Treating a manuscript-level novelty problem as a response-letter wording problem.  
**Recommended first move:** Diagnose *which level* of novelty the reviewer rejects, then map each novelty claim to evidence.

---

# E — Execute by gates

## Strategy
Write one sentence:

> "The paper is novel because ______ enables/reveals ______, which previous approaches cannot ______."

Try to falsify it.

## Structure
Build a reviewer-response map:

| Reviewer concern | Real diagnosis | Manuscript change | New evidence | Response |
|---|---|---|---|---|

## Artifact
Revise manuscript first, then draft the rebuttal around concrete changes.

## Polish
Only at the end optimize tone, concision, and wording.

---

## Teaching point

**The best rebuttal may begin by deciding not to rebut the reviewer's premise.**

Sometimes the reviewer is pointing to a story problem, evidence problem, or scientific-positioning problem that should be fixed upstream.
