# Worked Example 03 — Three-minute high-stakes institutional speech

## User request

> I have been invited as a young faculty representative to give a 3–4 minute speech at a university-wide development meeting attended by senior leadership. I am supposed to offer suggestions for the university's next five-year plan. Help me write the speech.

---

## Default execution-first response

A conventional assistant may immediately produce:

> "I have three suggestions: support young faculty, strengthen interdisciplinary research, and improve industry collaboration..."

This can be competent, safe, and forgettable.

---

# ROUTE analysis

## R — Reframe

**Surface task:** Write a 3–4 minute policy-suggestion speech.

**Possible real objective:** Contribute one credible institutional insight **and** allow senior decision-makers to form a high-quality impression of the speaker's judgment, perspective, and ability to connect personal experience with university-level strategy.

Why this inference?

A 3–4 minute format cannot seriously design a five-year policy system. Its practical function is likely to test:
- prioritization;
- judgment;
- strategic perspective;
- communication;
- credibility.

This is an inference, not a claim about hidden motives.

---

## O — Outcome

**Key stakeholder:** Senior university decision-makers; the wider audience matters secondarily.

**Success criterion:** After three minutes, the audience can remember:

1. one important strategic judgment;
2. why the speaker is unusually credible on this issue;
3. one feasible direction for institutional action;
4. the speaker as someone with perspective, substance, and proportion.

**Non-goal:** Produce a comprehensive five-year plan.

---

## U — Understand the game

### Attention constraint
Three minutes allows perhaps one core idea, not three unrelated agendas.

### Credibility
The speaker should choose a topic connected to firsthand research/teaching experience.

### Institutional proportion
The speech should:
- identify a real gap without humiliating existing units;
- propose a mechanism without pretending to redesign the university;
- show achievements without turning into a personal report;
- show ambition without sounding self-promotional.

### Functional vs literal task
"Give suggestions" is the literal format.  
"Demonstrate judgment through one suggestion" may be the more useful functional interpretation.

---

## T — Test

### Failure test 1
Could the speech contain perfectly reasonable suggestions that any faculty member could give?

If yes, it lacks identity.

### Failure test 2
Could the speech become a list of personal resource requests?

If yes, it lacks institutional perspective.

### Failure test 3
Could the speaker criticize the institution so directly that the strategic insight is lost in defensiveness?

If yes, the framing is inefficient.

### Alternative routes

**Route A — Three generic suggestions**  
Safe, broad, low memorability.

**Route B — One strategic thesis anchored in firsthand experience**  
Higher memorability and credibility.

**Route C — Personal achievement narrative**  
Memorable but risks self-promotion.

### Recommended route
**B**.

---

# Strategy Contract

**Surface task:** Write a short institutional policy speech.  
**Real objective:** Use one authentic strategic issue to demonstrate university-level judgment and credibility.  
**Key stakeholder:** Senior leadership/decision-makers.  
**Success criterion:** They remember one thesis and associate the speaker with insight + execution capability.  
**Strategic risk:** Spending the scarce three minutes on generic "three suggestions."  
**Recommended first move:** Choose one issue where the speaker has unique firsthand evidence and that maps to the institution's five-year competitiveness.

---

# E — Execute by gates

## Strategy
Create a single thesis, for example:

> "The university does not necessarily lack strong AI or strong disciplines; the next competitive step is to improve how efficiently they are coupled."

## Structure
A five-part flow:

1. **Who I am / why I have seen this problem**
2. **What progress I have observed internally**
3. **What external change makes this urgent**
4. **One structural bottleneck + one direction**
5. **Forward-looking close**

## Artifact
Write ~800 words at an appropriate speaking rate, with one memorable sentence.

## Polish
Optimize rhythm, pauses, spoken language, and proportion.

---

## Teaching point

The literal deliverable was a speech.

The strategic deliverable was:

> **a three-minute model of the speaker's judgment.**

Both matter—but the second determines how the first should be written.
